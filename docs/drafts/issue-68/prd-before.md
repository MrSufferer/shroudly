Spec: Deliver Shroudly as a production-operated Midnight Preprod application
# Problem Statement

At the start of this program, the repository presented a MixTogether Ethereum Sepolia demonstration built around Wagmi, Viem, Zama, Hardhat, EVM contracts, mocked browser flows, and a legacy Vercel preview. That implementation is not the accepted Midnight architecture and cannot substantiate a production claim for a private prize-savings application. In particular, the repository lacks a complete Midnight-native contract system, genuine finalized Preprod transactions, reproducible browser evidence against a deployed release, supported-wallet evidence, and an end-to-end maintainer runbook for provisioning and operating the required personal-account infrastructure.

Shroudly needs one supported architecture and one honest release claim. The immediate deliverable is a production-operated, publicly accessible Midnight Preprod release using valueless test assets, with real transaction and Playwright evidence. The same reviewed product mechanics and artifacts must support a fail-closed Mainnet deployment procedure, but the system must not accept real value or claim Mainnet readiness until the independently verifiable Mainnet Readiness Gate passes.

The implementation must also acknowledge that several required actions are controlled by a human maintainer: creating or configuring personal provider accounts, MFA, DNS, billing, secret values, signing authorities, independent audits and custodians, a real shielded asset and audited yield strategy, and legal approval. Engineering must automate everything safely possible, document every remaining human gate, and never put usable credentials or private state in GitHub, logs, test artifacts, or public evidence.

# Solution

Replace the active legacy stack with Shroudly, a Midnight-native private prize-savings application whose contracts are the sole authority for shielded test-asset issuance, principal accounting, time-weighted eligibility, randomness, prize funding, settlement, and claims. Keep asset/faucet, pool-accounting, randomness, and yield responsibilities explicit; qualify the conditional co-location experiment below before superseding ADR 0013, and deliver a React web experience that integrates a pinned Midnight wallet/provider stack, protects participant private state, supports encrypted recovery, and preserves participant-funded DUST submission when bounded sponsorship is unavailable.

Operate Preprod as a real public service: a static Vercel application, a stateless Render sponsor, GitHub Actions automation, Supabase operational storage and encrypted-backup storage, Resend authentication mail, and Better Stack monitoring/status communication. Use the providers' free tiers for Preprod with their limitations documented. Maintain separate environment-locked Preprod and Mainnet Vercel projects and domains. Mainnet uses paid service tiers and remains unable to accept contributions until all asset, yield, randomness, governance, emergency-custody, audit, legal, billing, authority-removal, and canary requirements have passed.

Prove the release through two deliberate test seams. The participant-journey seam runs the same externally observable journeys against deterministic local adapters and genuine Preprod adapters. The Compact contract-invariant seam exercises financial, authorization, randomness, privacy, and rollover behavior directly at the generated contract interface. Release evidence reuses the participant seam against the deployed Vercel site through a test-build-only wallet/prover bridge, adds one Lace-supported-wallet transaction, and requires network finality, indexer visibility, and a fresh ledger-state query for every transaction claimed as finalized.

Provide a comprehensive maintainer runbook that begins from empty personal accounts, names every required variable and authority without containing its value, covers free-tier Preprod setup and paid Mainnet prerequisites, and includes deployment, validation, evidence publication, monitoring, backup/restore, incident handling, reset recovery, key rotation, billing failure, rollback, and maintainer handoff procedures.

# Implementation Decisions

- **Product and migration:** Rename the active product to Shroudly throughout application metadata, accessible labels, documentation, screenshots, deployments, evidence, monitoring, and runbooks. Do not imply PoolTogether affiliation. Preserve the original MixTogether name only in clearly labeled legacy records. Accept the known Shroudly naming collision as an operator risk; brand clearance is not a Preprod gate, while the separate Mainnet legal gate remains mandatory.
- **One active architecture:** Replace rather than extend the legacy Ethereum stack. Remove Wagmi, Viem, Zama, Hardhat, EVM contracts, related environment variables, and EVM deployment steps from the active workspace and dependency graph. Preserve history in Git and useful disclosures under legacy documentation. The existing Sepolia contract and preview may remain reachable but are unsupported and noncanonical.
- **Midnight authority:** Midnight contracts are the sole authority for issuance, custody, Principal, Prize liabilities, commitments, eligibility, randomness results, settlement, and claims. Supabase, Render, GitHub Actions, Vercel, and telemetry are non-authoritative and cannot reconstruct private state or change financial outcomes.
- **Contract boundaries:** Retain ADR 0013 pending the co-location/custody experiment and authority review below. Enforce exact dependency bindings; backend trust and SDK batching are insufficient. Dependency replacement that affects economics requires a new audited deployment and participant-authorized migration.
- **Economic model:** Use shielded six-decimal `tMIX`, maximum issuance 10,000,000, a 1,000-per-epoch faucet claim, a separate automation allocation, a 1,000,000 initial Prize Reserve, and no more than 100 Simulated Yield per completed Preprod interval. Require new contributions of at least 1 `tMIX` and cap total outstanding Principal per stable deployment-scoped participant identity at 1,000 `tMIX`. Allow positive six-decimal withdrawals including full withdrawal and sub-1 remainders. Keep reserves segregated, enforce solvency, charge no protocol fee, and roll residue into future Prizes.
- **Eligibility and settlement:** Accrue Time-Weighted Principal continuously from confirmed contribution to confirmed withdrawal without locking Principal. Require a Disclosure Cohort of five eligible commitments. Publish a Winning Commitment, prove ownership and selection privately, consume a Claim Nullifier, and pay the Prize shielded. Roll empty, sub-threshold, cancelled, expired, and unclaimed Prizes forward.
- **Scheduling:** Keep Preprod weighting on fixed fifteen-minute UTC intervals, with commits ending three minutes before close and a five-minute reveal window after close. Maintain separate per-draw Prize liabilities and at least sixty usable claim minutes after finalized selection, with only a measured and explicitly accepted bounded network-time margin; extend only claims actually prevented by enforced settlement pauses by their blocked duration. Process valid historical draws in order without shifting boundaries or accepting late reveals. Continue current-interval admissions while earlier draws reveal or await claims. Mainnet timings remain gated.
- **Randomness:** Use a Preprod two-of-three commit/reveal set with isolated keys in GitHub Actions, Render, and an offline CLI recovery store, while explicitly documenting that one maintainer controls all three. Combine reveals canonically, allow permissionless finalization, and provide no fallback when threshold is missed. Mainnet requires audited randomness with independent operators.
- **Governance and emergencies:** Use two-of-three same-maintainer Preprod governance keys in separate encrypted offline stores. Require independent three-of-five timelocked governance, separate emergency custody, and independent randomness signers for Mainnet. Emergency actions are circuit-scoped, preserve withdrawals and already-valid claims, and cannot redirect value or select outcomes.
- **Deployer authority:** Retain only the narrowly enumerated Preprod deployer powers for at most twenty-four hours, disclose them in the app, alert before expiry, and disable new submissions plus open an incident if removal is late. Mainnet contributions remain technically disabled until deployer removal is finalized.
- **Participant privacy and recovery:** Keep identity/linkages, individual balances and amounts, weights, and winner identity private while publishing the minimum pool state required for verification. Store participant witness state encrypted locally. Require a Recovery Kit and successful local restore before first contribution. Support optional browser-encrypted remote backup through verified email/password plus TOTP, monotonic backup generations, explicit device handoff, accurate deletion disclosure, and read-only archival treatment after Preprod reset.
- **DUST sponsorship:** Use a stateless Sponsor Service with durable operational quota/reconciliation records, a capped hot credential, and allowlists. Bind idempotency to the original authorized action. An eight-second timeout leaves outcome pending/unknown; authenticated status lookup resumes across reload. Only authoritative confirmed non-submission permits funded fallback, and authorization changes or expiry require appropriate fresh approval. Preserve same-email rolling quota after account deletion until expiry through a disclosed keyed marker; unresolved reconciliation records remain separate.
- **Application boundary:** Evolve the existing Midnight provider-boundary pattern into the single participant application boundary used by local deterministic adapters and real network adapters. Default or unavailable providers must fail explicitly rather than fabricate success. The public build contains no automated bridge hooks.
- **Compatibility:** Pin an exact compiler, Compact runtime, ledger library, Midnight client, wallet connector, proof provider, indexer, and network profile. Qualify changes as a set; do not automatically accept semver-compatible updates. Record the profile in build output and evidence.
- **Environment isolation:** Use separate personal-account Vercel projects and domains for Preprod and Mainnet with build-time environment locks and persistent labels. Do not overwrite or reuse the legacy preview. Do not copy Preprod keys, balances, database rows, private state, or test-asset state into Mainnet.
- **Configuration authority:** Treat Vercel project environment variables as the canonical deployment configuration. Expose only a sanitized build-time configuration snapshot. Capture that snapshot in evidence, compare it with provider settings, and require redeployment plus fresh evidence after any canonical change. Do not describe this mutable configuration as cryptographically signed.
- **Service topology:** Host the static web application on Vercel, the stateless sponsor on Render, Preprod automation in scheduled/manually dispatched GitHub Actions, operational data and client-encrypted backups in Supabase, auth mail through Resend, and monitoring/status/redacted logs through Better Stack. Mainnet automation uses a paid always-on Render worker.
- **Data controls:** Apply versioned Supabase migrations and row-level security. Limit stored data to sponsorship quotas, automation jobs, Backup Accounts, and encrypted blobs. Do not use database rows as evidence of Principal, eligibility, winner, or settlement. Define backup and restore procedures with a twenty-four-hour Mainnet operational-data recovery point.
- **Telemetry:** Collect only aggregate privacy-safe operational measurements. Exclude wallet identifiers, commitments, nullifiers, backup object identifiers, witness or recovery secrets, complete transaction payloads, and IP-to-transaction correlations. Redact before log egress and use only short-lived random operational correlation identifiers.
- **Preprod reset behavior:** Run contract and indexer health checks every five minutes. Keep the last configuration visible until failure is detected and manually repaired, but disable submissions and show an outage once detected. Retire old private state read-only, redeploy and refaucet explicitly, and invalidate all prior release evidence until regenerated.
- **Release artifacts:** Promote only an immutable reviewed source tag and verified contract, proof, and web artifact hashes. Mainnet rebuilds or deploys reviewed inputs under independent configuration; source, dependency, or canonical configuration changes trigger a new Preprod qualification.
- **Mainnet fail-closed gate:** Do not deploy a token-accepting Mainnet application, treat NIGHT as a private asset, or promote `tMIX`, Simulated Yield, same-maintainer thresholds, or free-tier assumptions. Opening Mainnet contributions requires the named shielded asset, audited yield strategy, audited independent randomness, independent governance and emergency custody, independent security review, legal approval, paid services, deployer removal, and approved canary cap.
- **Credential handling:** Track safe variable names and placeholders in `.env.example`. Allow a maintainer to populate a gitignored, locally permission-restricted `.env.production.local`; transfer values into provider stores without printing them. Secret scanning covers source, docs, logs, screenshots, traces, manifests, and generated evidence.
- **Runbook:** Write a start-to-finish operational runbook for personal GitHub and provider accounts protected by password and authenticator MFA. Include account bootstrap; repository permissions; DNS; Vercel Preprod/Mainnet projects; Render sponsor and worker; Supabase database/auth/storage/RLS; Resend domain authentication; Better Stack monitors/status/responders; Midnight endpoints, contracts, keys, DUST, and roles; GitHub Actions secrets/environments; local credential preparation; deploy order; health and compatibility checks; evidence generation; release publication; authority handoff; rotations; revocation; backup/restore drills; incident handling; Preprod resets; billing/quota failure; provider migration; rollback/roll-forward; and future-maintainer transfer. For each free tier, record current operational limits and a validation method rather than assuming continued provider terms.
- **Human gates:** During decomposition, create separate `ready-for-human` issues for admin-only accounts, credentials, DNS, billing, production mail, legal review, independent security review, Mainnet asset and yield selection, independent custody, and any provider action unavailable to automation. Each issue names least-privilege scope, variable names, setup/validation, rotation/recovery, and expected sanitized evidence, never secret values.

# Testing Decisions

- **Seam 1 — participant journey:** Use the application's Midnight provider boundary as the highest practical test seam. The same user actions and observable state assertions run with deterministic local adapters in pull requests and with real Preprod wallet, proof, indexer, ledger, sponsor, backup, and deployment adapters in scheduled/manual evidence runs. Avoid test assertions against internal React structure when a participant-observable state is available.
- **Seam 2 — Compact contract invariant:** Exercise the generated Compact contract interface and deterministic ledger/reference runner directly for conditions too expensive, unsafe, private, or unreliable to create repeatedly through a browser. Keep the reference model independent enough to detect contract mistakes rather than copying the implementation line for line.
- **Local contract coverage:** Prove issuance caps and epoch nullifiers; contribution bounds; exact Principal withdrawal; mid-draw contributions; partial/full withdrawals; retained accrued weight; deterministic flooring and residue; reserve segregation and solvency response; yield checkpoint and replenishment bounds; empty/sub-threshold draws; commit cutoff; post-close reveals; contributor uniqueness; canonical reveal combination; threshold failure; no randomness fallback; permissionless finalization; unbiased weighted selection; private membership/ownership proofs; nullifier replay rejection; shielded payout; claim expiry; Prize Rollover; scoped pause; preserved exits/claims; deployer restrictions; migration authorization; and dependency pinning.

## Principal and eligibility

- One stable contract-proven participant identity within a deployment has at most 1,000 `tMIX` outstanding Principal. Deposits, reloads, recovery, and note splitting must preserve that identity. Identity is a deployment-scoped cryptographic credential, distinct from a wallet account or extension discovery ID. Fresh credentials can bypass the cap; this is not a per-wallet, unique-person, or Sybil-resistance guarantee.
- A new contribution is at least 1 `tMIX`, uses integer micro-units, and must fit remaining Principal capacity. Withdrawals accept any positive representable amount up to outstanding Principal, including full withdrawal or a remainder below 1 `tMIX`.
- Principal remains withdrawable regardless of prize outcome. Confirmed contribution and withdrawal boundaries determine weight; client-clock guesses do not establish inclusion.
- Calculate weight independently for each fixed UTC draw interval. Remaining Principal carries forward automatically while ordinary savers are offline, without per-draw check-ins or operator-held participant secrets. A fully withdrawn identity retains historical accrued weight for its previous interval and has zero future weight unless it contributes again.
- Five positive-weight commitments are the minimum Disclosure Cohort. Set a separate maximum capacity from measured compiler/proving/settlement results, and require support for more than five before qualification. Historical claims do not permanently occupy admission slots.

## Private-state lifecycle

- Scope encrypted state and the durable action journal by network, deployment, and participant identity, with verified wallet-account binding and isolation; an extension ID such as `lace` is not an account or participant identity. Recovery Kit export must include the complete material verified to restore genuine private actions, not just deployment or wallet identifiers.
- Unlock saved state using the participant-held Recovery Kit. Keep the decryption key in memory only. Email login, password reset, and TOTP restore access to remote ciphertext only.
- Before submission, atomically persist the recovery material and intent needed to reconcile the action after a crash. A failed durable write prevents submission. Exact serialization and generated-runtime integration require verification with genuine witness state.
- Observe the action through submission, finality, indexer visibility, and a fresh ledger-state query. Persist reconciled private state and the action outcome without losing the pending journal entry if final persistence fails.
- Restart enters reconciliation for unresolved actions. Do not infer failure from an observation timeout or successful decryption. If state cannot be established, explain the uncertainty and block affected new actions.
- Request browser storage persistence and qualify durability in supported Chrome. If storage cannot meet the requirement, block new contributions while preserving demonstrably safe exits. Do not promise recovery after explicit storage deletion or missing secret material.
- Use same-browser writer exclusion together with the persistent journal: release of a tab lock does not erase an unresolved action. Preprod supports one active browser installation and explicit transfer. Simultaneous copied-kit use across devices is unsupported; do not claim browser locking prevents it globally.

## Backup lifecycle

- Remote backup is optional. Generations advance through compare-and-swap updates; stale uploads do not overwrite current ciphertext or merge private state.
- Show an outdated remote backup warning while allowing actions from verified durable local state. Retry uploads when available and offer an updated local export.
- Restore older ciphertext into a reconciliation flow before affected actions. A successful decrypt does not prove freshness, and missing newer secrets are not assumed reconstructable from the ledger.
- Deletion removes the live Backup Account and ciphertext. Disclose actual verified provider-copy retention and the impossibility of deleting public ledger activity.
- Preserve the remaining rolling twenty-four-hour sponsorship quota on same-email re-enrollment through a minimal keyed email-derived marker until quota expiry. It remains personal data and must be disclosed. Retain unresolved sponsor reconciliation records separately. Avoid retaining raw email or transaction payloads in the quota marker; another email can still bypass a per-email limit.

## Sponsor API and action outcomes

- Submission binds an idempotency key to the original account, deployment, and authorized action. A repeated key with a different action is rejected. Secrets and complete transaction payloads never enter logs or evidence.
- Authenticated status lookup distinguishes `confirmed-not-submitted`, `pending-or-unknown`, `submitted`, and `finalized`. It returns only the information authorized for that request; the exact versioned wire schema remains an implementation deliverable.
- The eight-second request timeout is a UI observation boundary, not evidence of failure. Preserve the action across reload and resume lookup of its original request.
- Only authoritative confirmed non-submission permits a participant-funded fallback. Preserve the intended action; obtain fresh approval when authorization has expired or funding changes require it. Never silently modify an approved financial action.
- Missing/expired records and absence from a lagging indexer mean unknown. If submitted, observe the original transaction. Financial success requires network finality, indexer visibility, and fresh ledger confirmation.
- Retain minimal reconciliation records for unresolved requests. Operators may investigate but cannot override ledger truth or declare a retry safe without authoritative evidence. Show the pending status and support route.

## Draw and claim lifecycle

- Draw boundaries stay on the fixed UTC fifteen-minute schedule; late callers do not shift them. A draw closes weighting before required randomness reveal and selection.
- Each settled draw has its own Winning Commitment, Prize liability, claim nullifier, and published claim deadline. Earlier unclaimed Prizes do not prevent subsequent draws or become available to pay another Prize.
- Preserve existing two-of-three randomness, canonical contributor ordering, no fallback, cohort rollover, reserve segregation, and one-shot private claim requirements.
- Guarantee sixty usable minutes after finalized selection. Permit only a conservative, explicitly bounded network-time margin measured in the pinned-runtime experiment and separately accepted as a release constant; no bound is assumed. Extend the deadline only by the actual duration of an enforced settlement pause that prevents the affected claim. Unrelated pauses do not extend it; operators cannot grant arbitrary grace periods. Record and prove the relevant pause intervals under the existing bounded authority rules.
- Historical draws remain finalizable after an outage if original interval eligibility and timely randomness commitments/reveals are valid. Process them in chronological order while preserving UTC boundaries, segregated liabilities, and at-most-once yield allocation. The bounded catch-up implementation must support resumable work rather than require one unbounded transaction.
- Missing the original reveal threshold cancels that draw and rolls its Prize forward. Late processing cannot admit late reveals or alternate randomness. Contributions during an earlier draw's reveal/claim period accrue only in the currently open interval; they cannot change closed historical weights.

## Contract feasibility and authority

- Treat current exact package/tool pins as a candidate profile, not a qualified stack. Current docs and skill examples do not establish compatibility with pinned declarations, generated artifacts, or the deployed network. No blind upgrades.
- Prefer an experiment co-locating pool accounting, randomness verification, and Simulated Yield accounting in one contract with source modules. Asset separation requires demonstrated custody enforcement. SDK batching and source imports do not authenticate cross-contract results. ADR 0013 remains accepted; supersede it only after a viable result and explicit authority/upgrade review preserving opt-in migration.
- Enforce registered contributor/draw bindings, randomness thresholds, one-shot yield allocation and actual shielded custody in contract constraints. Backend claims or arbitrary caller-supplied results cannot establish financial truth.
- Ledger state is public even without `export`. Use genuine commitments and constrained private proofs for balances, amounts, weights and ownership. Audit public ledger, circuit inputs/outputs, proof transcripts and linkages; do not publish weights to meet performance targets.
- Demonstrate exact weighted settlement with offline savers, more than five eligible credentials, historical catch-up and safe withdrawals while historical draws remain unresolved. Preserve every affected historical weight and segregated liability; backlog alone cannot be an exit condition. Commitments alone do not supply automation witness openings.
